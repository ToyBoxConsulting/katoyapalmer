Thought

01

Gone, not forgotten

Lucky Chinese is gone now, and I wasn't ready to let it go.

From the outside looking in, it looked like a lot of the restaurants I enjoy: not much happening on the outside, really quiet on the inside, and a lot of surprise to be had. My good friend Erik Kalligraphy took me there for the first time, on July 3, 2025, and he says it's some of the best Chinese food in the city.

A triple rent hike pushed them out of their first place on 4th Ave S in 2022. They came back in 2024, a little farther down the same street, and now they're gone for good. This week I finally told the story, with the video and the photos from that day.

For the simple fact that it's gone now, it is super sad.

Thank you, Vanishing Seattle, for keeping the story.

Funding is coming for King County youth programs

I serve on King County's Local Advisory Team for the Community Reinvestment Program, working with United Way of King County to decide where this Washington State Department of Commerce funding goes. The grant supports violence prevention for young people ages 14 to 24: mentorship, safe spaces, youth leadership, life and job skills, and culturally responsive programs. We're prioritizing by-and-for organizations serving Black, Indigenous, Latine, Asian and Pacific Islander youth, with budgets under $2 million. Grants run $25,000 to $48,000, and the RFP opens in the coming weeks.

I don't ever have to buy another can

SZND by Katoya

Cream of chicken and cream of mushroom, from scratch

I've used Campbell's my entire life. Last year, when they were going through their PR crisis, I was a little confused and didn't really know what to do. So I just made them myself.

Cream of chicken is just a roux and broth, with cream poured in at the end. Cream of mushroom starts with butter, onion and garlic. I used about half of what I made and froze the rest. Just not in the mason jars, that was my one mistake: move them to a different dish before they go in the freezer.

Lipstick on My Fork

Lucky Chinese, Seattle

Four and a half kisses, dedicated to my good friend Erik Kalligraphy.

The first thing Erik recommended was the strawberry juice. One sip and I was sold, and I really wish there was a bigger cup. The hot and sour soup was complimentary, and you don't find that much anymore. Erik went with the sweet and sour, I went with the General Tso's, and we ended up trading bites: a perfect chicken crunch, a glaze not too sweet and not too sticky, and the spice was perfect.

My first table at Coming Home

Friday night was my first time hosting a table at Coming Home, and it was a table of family.

It was also my first time ever hosting a table at a gala or fundraising event, and it happened with the support of family. My cousin, Brandon Roy, sponsored the table. He is selfless when it comes to taking care of community, and he afforded me the opportunity to host a table of family for the first time ever.

Coming Home is The Sophia Way's evening celebrating the journeys of women to a home of their own, at the Hilton Bellevue. Learn about The Sophia Way

Druski on SNL

I love SNL no matter what, and I always watch the sketches. But for Druski, I definitely wanted to show up and support. He hosted on Saturday, November 7, the first host in the show's history whose career was built online.

It was great to see him in his element. SNL is exactly where he's supposed to be, and without the internet, who knows where he'd be today. If you follow Druski, nothing surprised you: they gave him his trademarks, and it was hilarious. It was great to see legends like Dave Chappelle, Chris Rock and Michael B. Jordan there supporting him.

The only thing I wished for was more of him and Marcello Hernandez. In my heart, I was waiting for them to get together. They need to do more sketches, honestly they need to do a movie, and I need to see more of it.
