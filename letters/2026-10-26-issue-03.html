Thought

01

Spot the red flag

Nothing dramatic happened at this board meeting. That was the problem.

This week ToyBox played a game. A monthly board meeting: the finance report is two days late again, the treasurer says the bookkeeper "has it handled," nobody else has login access, and the vote is unanimous with no questions.

  • 🚩 Late reports, again
  • 🚩 One person holds the keys
  • 🚩 No questions asked

The answer was all three. Late reports mean the board decides without the numbers. Twelve loyal years is not a control. And a unanimous vote with no questions isn't trust; it skips the board's duty of care.

This one means a lot to me. I'm a board treasurer, an interim COO and a former nonprofit COO, and I've seen this scenario more than once. "The bookkeeper has it handled" plus a board approving documents it has never seen is a host of internal control issues. It's something I consult on and advise against when I'm helping a client.

When I manage a client's finances, I make sure I'm never the only one with access, and every decision I make goes through leadership and the board first, as it should. I've also lived the worst version: in one organization, the person controlling the finances, the board reports and the bookkeeping was misappropriating funds. Working through that took a long time, and it taught me much of what I know about finance and restricted funds. I use it in my work today, and it's why I love to teach and inform people about this.

It's not that you don't trust people. It's that you put things in place so everything is done with fidelity, especially with public dollars.

Even when it isn't public money, keeping integrity strong still matters. The fix is small: two people with account access, a monthly bank reconciliation a board member reviews, and at least one finance question at every meeting.

From the writing desk

02

Revisiting Late Bloom

Last August I published a short story called Late Bloom: Noa, a tired executive and single mom, a lavender bath, a garden, and a hummingbird that shows up right when she chooses herself.

Late Bloom came out of a hard stretch. Just over a year ago I had lost my job and was trying to get back to myself, and writing has always been where I go. I wrote and read stories as a kid.

Noa is fiction, but she loosely mirrors me and other women in my life going through our own late bloom. I'm in my mid-40s, a Xennial, and a lot of this is happening all around me. I first wanted it to be a book. It became a short story, and then my Five Windows series took over.

I tried hard to make it feel like fiction, but it's really a picture of life.

A year later I see what I couldn't then. Noa has what I've dreamed of, a successful executive career and financial security, and she still deals with the real stuff, like good guys gone bad.

What's next? I'm not sure yet. I haven't shared it much, so I want to see what it does as a short story. If you know a place that features short fiction, hit reply.

My Thanksgiving apple pie

I cook my apples first, soft and smothered, like a turnover.

Blame the fast-food pies I grew up on: the Hostess pie I microwaved after school (burned my tongue every time), the old fried McDonald's pie, and, when I was pregnant with my son, a McDonald's apple pie crushed into a vanilla shake. Lattice on top, cooled filling so the crust stays crisp.

Off the clock

07

Horror movie season: the dump 🎃

Halloween is close, so I've been on a horror run.

🔪 Crystal Lake, the new series

The new Friday the 13th series. I watched it this weekend. Super good, whatever the mixed reviews say. It showed me Mrs. Voorhees before Jason died, sent me back to the original, and left room for another season.

⛺ Friday the 13th (the original)

Rewatched it right after Crystal Lake. I'd forgotten a lot. Watching it again, I appreciated how much of the movie's opening the Crystal Lake finale replicates.

🎃 Rob Zombie's Halloween (2007)

The one that starts with Michael as a kid and follows him out of the asylum. Super disturbing to watch such a young kid do all of that, but I love Rob Zombie's take, and this one was no different. My son called it trash.

🛏️ A Nightmare on Elm Street 1, and 4: The Dream Master

Part 4 is the one with the waterbed scene. Classics. I had them on as background noise while I worked, just to get my Freddy in for the season.

🧛🏾 Vampires vs. the Bronx

Kids versus vampires, and it's really about gentrification. Watched it over dinner. Cute, and deeper than you'd expect: gentrification through the eyes of the neighborhood kids, turned into a horror movie.

🪓 Frailty (2001)

Bill Paxton directed it and stars in it, with Matthew McConaughey. An old classic. I didn't finish it this time because I've seen it so many times. I do like a religious or psychological thriller.

💍 Bride of Chucky

Funny as hell. To me, anyway. It's very gory, but once you understand how these movies work, it's a different watch. I picked this one because I play Dead by Daylight, and I like to be the bride for real.

📖 Evil Dead Burn

Not too impressive to me, and I've been a fan since Evil Dead 1 and 2, Army of Darkness and Ash. Evil Dead Rise was cool. This one felt over the top. I finished it, but I didn't want to.

Honorable mention: the stick-figure zombie survival series on TikTok, where every episode is a different place to ride out the apocalypse. KFC, the airport, the farm, jail, and last night, Costco. I like it because it almost feels like it's teaching you what to really do. It's a whole genre now, with lots of creators; many credit @stickocalypse_shorts as the spark.

Fave game: Dead by Daylight. My favorite video game, and I've played it for about six years. It's cool to watch it change and evolve. I don't play killer often, but when I do, it's Chucky or the Bride. As a survivor I'm a Dwight, and since Lara Croft came out, I've been playing her pretty intently.

What are you watching this October? Hit reply with your scariest pick.
