Five Windows. One Stage.

On the workshop the essays built

Katoya Raquell Palmer · April 29, 2026 · 3 min read
Originally published on Substack, April 29, 2026.

I wrote five essays this year about the cracks in our systems. On May 6, I will show nonprofit leaders the tools to fix them.

That sentence still feels strange to type.

Five Windows into a Fragile Economy was the diagnosis — housing, community safety, nonprofit fragility, technology and access, inclusive growth. I wrote those essays in 2025 from a desk I had just rebuilt during a year I had not planned for: unemployed at the same moment Black women’s unemployment climbed past 6.7%, rebuilding ToyBox full-time, holding three board seats, and watching the systems I had spent two decades inside continue to fail people who looked like me.

The essays were the diagnosis. The workshop is the prescription.

When the Washington State Nonprofit Conference selected my proposal — Beyond Compliance Theater: Building Accountability Systems That Strengthen (Not Just Monitor) Performance — out of more than 130 submissions, I sat with that for a long minute. Not because I doubted the work. Because I knew exactly which version of myself had earned the right to teach it.

The Katoya in 2008 with a Section 8 voucher in her hand and a 24-week micro preemie in the NICU just 2 years prior did not know what fiduciary duty was.

The Katoya in 2016 fixing an alternator off eBay with a YouTube video and a neighbor’s help did not know what risk transfer meant.

The Katoya in 2022 stepping into a COO seat at a $12M social-justice nonprofit in crisis learned both — fast — under conditions no leader should have to learn anything.

The Katoya writing this learned to name what she had been absorbing all along.

That is what the workshop is. Not training. Translation. From “they didn’t follow through” to what in the system’s design made this outcome predictable. From “we need more training” to what is the structural gap. From “we made an exception out of compassion” to the standard holds — what support does this person need to meet it.

The frameworks I will teach on May 6 — the Five Windows Diagnostic, the D/E/C Power Framework, the Decision Rights Matrix, the Case Comparison Method, the 90/180/365 Horizon Sort, the NOT-Do List — are not theory I read in a textbook. They are tools I built because I needed them. Because my organization needed them. Because the people closest to the ground always need them and almost never get them.

I built them in spreadsheets at midnight. I tested them with co-directors in distress. I refined them across nine workshops with one client and four with another. I gave them names so other leaders could pick them up and carry them home.

That is what 75 minutes will be: handing tools across the screen, watching nonprofit leaders pick them up, asking each one to commit to a specific change in 90 days with their own name on it.

Some of those leaders will be running organizations whose programs I once accessed. Some will be running organizations I once helped to design. All of them are carrying weight that was never theirs to carry alone.

The workshop is not for the version of me that needed those programs. The workshop is for the leaders I serve now — and for the systems they are responsible for redesigning so the next Katoya does not have to fix her own alternator on her way to a job at the Y while figuring out whether her preemie’s immune system can handle a crowded bus.

I have been writing my whole life toward this stage.

The essays said: here is where it breaks.

The workshop says: here is how you build it differently.

Both are true. Both are necessary. Neither is enough on its own.

Inclusion is not charity. It is infrastructure.

And infrastructure requires builders.

— Katoya
